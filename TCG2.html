CSAT Project

TCG²

Time-Constrained Guidance for Multiple Unmanned Vehicles with Convergence Guarantees

Principal Investigator Kun Wang
Funding €10,000
Duration 2025–2027
Funding agency Universidad Carlos III de Madrid — Programa Propio de Investigación y Transferencia

This project develops reliable and computationally efficient guidance methods that enable multiple unmanned vehicles to reach their designated destinations at a specified common arrival time. By combining trajectory shaping, geometric optimal control and rigorous convergence analysis, the project seeks to bridge the gap between theoretical guidance guarantees and practical multi-UAV operations.

Background and motivation

Coordinated missions involving several unmanned vehicles, such as cooperative surveillance, formation assembly and synchronized operations, often require precise timing as well as accurate motion control. For example, three fixed-wing UAVs may depart from different positions and headings yet need to arrive simultaneously at three distinct vertices of a triangular formation. Existing guidance techniques present important trade-offs. Linearization-based methods are computationally attractive but may not fully capture nonlinear vehicle dynamics. Nonlinear control approaches can account for these dynamics but may demand excessive control effort or difficult time-to-go estimates. Learning-based methods can be effective but typically offer limited interpretability and formal convergence guarantees. This project addresses these limitations through a trajectory-shaping framework designed for time-constrained coordination.

Our approach

The research represents vehicle trajectories using a small set of guidance parameters, reducing a nonlinear guidance problem to the solution of algebraic equations without relying on linearized motion models. Geometric optimal control principles are used to identify energy-efficient trajectory structures, while a mathematical analysis of the parameter solution space provides the basis for convergence guarantees and mission-feasibility assessment before execution.

Three lines of research

The research plan is organized into three complementary work packages (WP).

1

Energy-aware trajectory shaping

WP1. Formulate kinematic models with nonholonomic and arrival-time constraints, derive necessary optimality conditions, and incorporate geometric insights, such as trajectory curvature and control effort, into parameterized guidance laws. The aim is to obtain computationally tractable, energy-conscious suboptimal solutions.

2

Convergence guarantees and real-time algorithms

WP2. Characterize the solution space of guidance parameters, investigate existence and uniqueness, and derive explicit parameter bounds. These results will support formal convergence analysis, a priori checks of feasible arrival conditions, and efficient algorithms suitable for real-time implementation.

3

Simulation and flight validation

WP3. Assess the guidance algorithms through numerical and Monte Carlo simulations incorporating vehicle dynamics, disturbances and sensor noise, followed by 3D demonstrations using Unity 3D or FlightGear. Flight experiments are underway to evaluate practical performance on fixed-wing UAV platforms.

Outcomes and impact

The project is expected to deliver mathematical convergence results, real-time guidance algorithms, comparative simulation studies and experimental UAV demonstrations. More broadly, the research aims to improve the reliability, timing accuracy and energy efficiency of coordinated autonomous-vehicle missions in aerospace engineering and robotics.
